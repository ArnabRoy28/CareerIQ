Section III — Proposed System
4-Stage ML Pipeline
01
Data Collection
1,200 CSE/IT student profiles. 15 attributes across 4 categories: Academic, Technical, Core Subjects, Soft Skills. 6 career labels. Mix of survey data and synthetic records.
02
CFS Feature Selection
Correlation-based Feature Selection with Best-First search evaluates feature-class correlation vs inter-feature redundancy. Reduces 15 → 9 features (40% reduction).
03
Model Training
80/20 stratified split (train=960, test=240, random_state=42). Three classifiers: RF (200 trees, Gini), SVM (RBF, C=10, γ=scale, OvR), KNN (k=7, Euclidean). 5-fold CV.
04
Flask Deployment
Real-time career suggestions via Flask web interface. Sub-second response times. Accessible in resource-constrained educational environments without specialised hardware.
Section III-D — CFS Formula
Merit Score Equation
Merit(S) = (k × r̄cf) / √(k + k(k−1) × r̄ff)
kNumber of features in subset S
r̄cfAverage Pearson correlation between each feature and the class label
r̄ffAverage inter-feature Pearson correlation (redundancy penalty)

CFS maximises predictive correlation with the target career class while penalising redundancy between selected features. Best-First search was used as the search strategy, ultimately selecting 9 of 15 features.

Section III-C — Normalisation
Min-Max Scaling (Eq. 1)
xnorm = (x − xmin) / (xmax − xmin)

All continuous features (CGPA, Logical Reasoning, Problem-Solving) are normalised to [0, 1]. Ordinal features are divided by their maximum value (3 for skill levels). Binary features remain as 0 or 1.

Section V-C — Limitations
Known Constraints

1. Dataset size: 1,200 profiles is small for a 6-class problem; ≥5,000 profiles from diverse institutions would improve generalisation.

2. Feature granularity: Internship Experience and Certification Count are coarse — they do not capture the quality or relevance of experience.

3. Static model: No real-time job market integration; recommendations may lag behind industry hiring trends.

4. Single-label output: Many students could succeed in multiple roles; multi-label or ranked probability output would be more realistic.

Section VI-B — Future Work
Planned Extensions

Deep Learning: Replace RF with attention-based or MLP architectures on larger multi-institutional datasets.

Real-Time Market Data: Connect to job portal APIs to dynamically weight recommendations by industry hiring demand.

Multi-Label Classification: Output ranked probability distributions across all 6 career categories simultaneously.

XAI / SHAP: Incorporate SHAP-based feature contribution analysis for personalised recommendation explanations.

Adaptive Profiling: Longitudinal student profiling that updates recommendations as students progress through their academic programme.
